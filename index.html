The Challenge

Ghana's Most Valuable
Asset is Being Destroyed

Ghana generates over 1.1 million tonnes of plastic waste annually, much of which ends up on our beaches — harming marine life, threatening fishing communities, and driving away tourists.

Existing cleanup efforts are fragmented, underfunded, and temporary. Volunteer campaigns come and go. Without permanent enforcement or employment infrastructure, nothing changes.

Ghana doesn't need another cleanup campaign. Ghana needs a permanent System.

🌊
Plastic Pollution Crisis 1.1M tonnes of plastic waste generated annually. Rivers and drains channel it directly into the ocean
🐟
Collapsing Marine Ecosystems Declining fish harvest and deteriorating biodiversity threaten coastal livelihoods for hundreds of thousands
📉
Lost Tourism Revenue Polluted beaches drive away international visitors, costing Ghana significant economic opportunity
👷🏾‍♂️
Youth Unemployment Coastal youth communities lack stable, dignified employment, A crisis that a permanent beach workforce can directly solve
Our Approach

Three Pillars of
Permanent Protection

01

EPA Beach Protection Task Force

A dedicated enforcement and coordination unit within the EPA with full legal authority to patrol beaches, prosecute illegal dumpers, monitor water quality, and coordinate coastal protection nationwide.

Policy & Enforcement
02

National Beach Employment Program

A permanent, paid workforce of Beach Cleaners, Beach Security Personnel, and Supervisors deployed across 50+ major beach locations. Workers receive formal training and a nationally recognized certification.

1,000+ Direct Jobs
03

Coastal Infrastructure Development

Public washroom facilities, strategically placed waste collection bins, and professional-grade cleaning equipment installed at all open beaches, transforming dumping grounds into national assets.

Phase One: 50 Beaches

Who We Are

Born From the Community,
Built for the Nation

We are a grassroots community initiative born from direct observation of the crisis on Ghana's beaches. Our strength is not in organizational size but in community trust, local knowledge, and the quality of the solution we are proposing.

We actively seek partnerships with government, established NGOs, and international donors to bring this vision to life. We represent the communities most affected by beach pollution — and we are ready to work.

Our approach is rooted in economic sustainability: by tying environmental protection to permanent employment, we create incentives for communities to become guardians of their own coastline. When people's livelihoods depend on clean beaches, beaches stay clean.
